# GPA Study Hub — Current Design Direction

## Product stance

The product should feel like a focused academic workspace, not a sci-fi dashboard or a generic AI app. Its job is to help a student decide what to do next, complete that action, and return to the rest of the semester with less cognitive load.

## 2026-informed principles

The new interface follows calm design, strategic minimalism, progressive disclosure, contextual AI, unified search/commands, adaptive layouts, and WCAG 2.2 fundamentals. Decorative 3D, glassmorphism, giant hero art, constant glow, and ambiguous status labels are intentionally excluded from the primary workflows.

## Art direction: “Ink & Highlight”

The canvas is a warm editorial paper tone. A deep ink-blue rail provides orientation. Cobalt is the main action color, acid-lime marks success and progress, and coral marks risk or deadlines. Surfaces are mostly flat, lightly bordered, and generously spaced. Typography uses a strong display face for short hierarchy and a highly legible sans-serif for body copy. Large headings are reserved for page entry points; cards remain compact and task-oriented.

## Experience model

The shell has three layers: a persistent workspace rail on desktop, an adaptive top bar with a command/search entry point, and the current task surface. The dashboard is a “Today” view: one primary focus, a few next actions, measurable progress, and notices. Secondary tools are reachable by command palette, search, or contextual links rather than being displayed all at once.

AI is presented as contextual help inside study tasks, not as a glowing separate destination that competes with the student’s immediate goal.

## Responsive behavior

Desktop uses a 240–260px rail and bounded content canvas. Tablet collapses the rail into a compact top navigation. Mobile uses a bottom dock with five destinations and keeps primary actions in the thumb zone. Content is reflowed and reprioritized, not merely shrunk.
